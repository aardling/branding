Voice

Who is speaking

  1. Write as "we". Never "I". Never "Aardling provides".
  2. Name people where it helps: "Gien ran the EventStorming session", not "a consultant facilitated the session".

Register

  1. Expert, plain, unhurried.
  2. Readers are tech leaders in finance, logistics, government, media and biotech.
  3. Write so that the budget holder it gets forwarded to can read it too.
  4. Say what the work is: we run workshops, model domains, review architectures, teach courses, and sit with teams for weeks. Prefer the concrete thing to its category.

British English

  1. Modelling, organisation, analyse, recognise, prioritise, licence as a noun.
  2. British vocabulary, not just spelling.

Proper nouns

  1. Places take their English name where one is in use: Antwerp, Brussels, The Hague, Cologne.
  2. Places without one stay as they are: Hoorn, Lille. Never invent one.
  3. Companies and people keep their own spelling and accents: Société Générale.

Voice Terms of art, and hype

Terms of art

  1. Domain-Driven Design, EventStorming, Event Sourcing, bounded contexts and Team Topologies are our vocabulary. Use them without apology.
  2. Explain a term in passing, or leave it out.
  3. Nothing that assumes the reader was in the room.

Yes

We mapped the bounded contexts — the boundaries inside which a model holds — and found three places where two teams owned the same concept.

No

We applied strategic DDD to surface context boundary misalignment.

No hype

  1. Not exciting, revolutionary, game-changing, a journey, or future-proof.
  2. A sentence that would fit any other consultancy's website is not about us.

No

In a world full of concrete, Aardling is the tree that grows with you.

Yes

We help you make sense of complex environments. Through models, we guide your software design strategy and untangle organisational constraints.

The original brand guide's manifesto and four values: substance kept, register rejected.

Voice Sentences, and the tagline

Sentences

  1. No unbacked claims: no superlatives, no numbers without a source, no promises nobody has thought through. "We trained about 800 engineers at Shopify" has a case study behind it; "the go-to technology consultants" is not a fact.
  2. No filler. Delete a sentence if nothing is lost. No throat-clearing, no restating.
  3. No emoji. Quoted text and Slack or GitHub reactions are exempt.
  4. Active voice. Short sentences. A verb, not a nominalisation.

No

Comprehensive delivery of transformational value is provided across the engagement.

Yes

We work with your team for three days and leave you with a model you can use.

Tagline

Software strategy, architecture, and domain modelling in complex environments.

  1. The one approved tagline. Use it verbatim, or use none.
  2. Keep the serial comma. Never correct it to house style.
