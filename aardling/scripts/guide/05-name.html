Aardling

  1. Capital A, the rest lower case, one word. There is no second accepted form.
  2. Everywhere: headlines, body copy, navigation, email, decks, social posts, and a sentence that begins with it.
  3. In prose, never aardling, never AARDLING, never AardLing.

Machine identifiers

  1. Identifiers stay lower case.
  2. The spelling rule governs text a reader sees, not identifiers.

Logotype

  1. The mark sets AARDLING in capitals with the double-A ligature. That is lettering.
  2. Never set the name in capitals anywhere else: not in a heading, a navigation item or a slide title.

Mark and word

  1. The mark is a drawing. The name is text.
  2. A slide carries AARDLING once, as artwork, and Aardling everywhere the reader reads.

Company names

  1. Use an entity name only where the law or an accountant needs it: an imprint, a contract, an invoice, terms and conditions, a privacy policy, a VAT line.
  2. Everywhere else the brand name stands alone.
  3. Never use an entity name in a headline, a deck, a byline or body copy.

Yes

Aardling ran the modelling workshop.

No

Aardling BV ran the modelling workshop.

Registered addresses

  1. Canonical. Transcribe the street lines exactly.
  2. Place names take their English form where one exists: Antwerp, not Antwerpen.
  3. Aardling BV and Aardling Belgium BV differ only by bus number.
  4. An address appears only where an entity name does.
  5. Registration and VAT numbers are not carried here. Take them from the register or finance.
