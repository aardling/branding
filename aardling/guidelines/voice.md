# Voice

The repository's general voice rules are the floor. This file says how Aardling stands on it.

## Who is speaking

**We.** Aardling is a group of consultants, and the writing says so. Not "I" — that belongs to
a personal blog, not to the brand. Not "Aardling provides", which turns a room full of people
into an institution issuing statements.

Name the people where naming them helps. "Gien ran the EventStorming session" is better than
"a consultant facilitated the session".

## Register

Expert, plain, unhurried. Our readers are tech leaders in finance, logistics, government,
media and biotech. They are technically literate and they are busy, and much of what we write
gets forwarded to someone who controls a budget. Write so that both can read it.

**British spelling.** Modelling, organisation, analyse, recognise, prioritise, licence as a
noun. Never modeling, never organizations.

**Say what the work is.** We run workshops, model domains, review architectures, teach courses,
and sit with teams for weeks. Every one of those is a concrete thing that happened on a date.
Prefer the concrete thing to the category it belongs to.

## Terms of art, used carefully

Domain-Driven Design, EventStorming, Event Sourcing, bounded contexts, Team Topologies — these
are our vocabulary and we use them without apology. But the reader may not share our
background, so a term earns its place by being either explained in passing or unnecessary.

> Yes: We mapped the bounded contexts — the boundaries inside which a model holds — and found
> three places where two teams owned the same concept.
>
> No: We applied strategic DDD to surface context boundary misalignment.

No in-jokes. No conference shorthand. Nothing that assumes the reader was in the room.

## What we do not write

**No hype.** Not exciting, not revolutionary, not game-changing, not a journey, not
future-proof, not next-level, not a partner on your transformation. If a sentence would survive
being pasted into any other consultancy's website, it is not about us.

> No: In a world full of concrete, Aardling is the tree that grows with you.
>
> Yes: We help you make sense of complex environments. Through models, we guide your software
> design strategy, untangle organisational constraints, and enable flow.

**No unbacked claims.** No superlatives, no numbers without a source, no promises nobody has
thought through. "We trained about 800 engineers at Shopify" is a fact with a case study behind
it. "The go-to technology consultants" is not a fact at all.

**No filler.** If a sentence can be deleted and nothing is lost, delete it. No throat-clearing
before the point, no restating what was just said in different words.

## Sentences

Active voice. Short sentences. A verb where a nominalisation tempts.

> No: Comprehensive delivery of transformational value is provided across the engagement.
>
> Yes: We work with your team for three days and leave you with a model you can use.

## Notes on the source

Hartstikke's brand guide carries a manifesto and four values written in a register this file
rejects — "the tree that grows with you", "go-to technology consultants", "future-proof",
"make your technology future-proof". The substance behind those values is right and is kept:
we know the technology, we adapt to the team rather than imposing a method, we build a
foundation the client can carry on with, and we have the track record to draw on. The register
is not kept.
