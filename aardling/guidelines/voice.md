# Voice

The repository's general voice rules apply. This file specialises them for Aardling.

## Who is speaking

1. Write as "we". Aardling is a group of consultants.
2. Never "I". Never "Aardling provides".
3. Name people where naming them helps: "Gien ran the EventStorming session", not "a
   consultant facilitated the session".

## Register

1. Expert, plain, unhurried.
2. Readers are tech leaders in finance, logistics, government, media and biotech: technically
   literate and busy.
3. Write so that the budget holder it gets forwarded to can read it too.
4. Say what the work is: we run workshops, model domains, review architectures, teach courses,
   and sit with teams for weeks. Prefer the concrete thing to its category.

## British English

1. British spelling: modelling, organisation, analyse, recognise, prioritise, licence as a
   noun. Never modeling, never organizations.
2. British vocabulary, not just spelling.

## Proper nouns

1. Places take their English name where one is already in use: Antwerp, not Antwerpen;
   Brussels, not Brussel or Bruxelles; The Hague, Cologne, Munich, Lisbon.
2. Places with no English name stay as they are: Hoorn, Lille, Tabakvest. Never invent one.
3. Companies and people keep their own spelling. Never anglicise, never strip accents:
   Société Générale, not Societe Generale.
4. Aardling is a Dutch word and stays one.
5. Spell a person's name the way they spell it.

## Terms of art

1. Domain-Driven Design, EventStorming, Event Sourcing, bounded contexts and Team Topologies
   are our vocabulary. Use them without apology.
2. Explain a term in passing, or leave it out.
3. No in-jokes, no conference shorthand, nothing that assumes the reader was in the room.

> Yes: We mapped the bounded contexts — the boundaries inside which a model holds — and found
> three places where two teams owned the same concept.
>
> No: We applied strategic DDD to surface context boundary misalignment.

## Not written

1. No hype: not exciting, revolutionary, game-changing, a journey, future-proof, next-level,
   or a partner on your transformation.
2. A sentence that would fit any other consultancy's website is not about us.
3. No unbacked claims: no superlatives, no numbers without a source, no promises nobody has
   thought through. "We trained about 800 engineers at Shopify" has a case study behind it;
   "the go-to technology consultants" is not a fact.
4. No filler. Delete a sentence if nothing is lost. No throat-clearing, no restating.
5. No emoji: not in headings, body copy, bullets, or to soften a sentence.
6. Exceptions to 5: quoted text stands as written; emoji reactions on Slack or GitHub are not
   brand writing.

> No: In a world full of concrete, Aardling is the tree that grows with you.
>
> Yes: We help you make sense of complex environments. Through models, we guide your software
> design strategy, untangle organisational constraints, and enable flow.

> No: We help you make sense of complex environments 🌳
>
> Yes: We help you make sense of complex environments.

## Sentences

1. Active voice.
2. Short sentences.
3. A verb, not a nominalisation.

> No: Comprehensive delivery of transformational value is provided across the engagement.
>
> Yes: We work with your team for three days and leave you with a model you can use.

## Boilerplate

1. One approved tagline. Use it verbatim, or use none:

   > Software strategy, architecture, and domain modelling in complex environments.

2. Keep its serial comma. Do not correct it to house style anywhere it is reproduced.

## Notes on the source

1. Hartstikke's brand guide has a manifesto and four values in a rejected register: "the tree
   that grows with you", "go-to technology consultants", "make your technology future-proof".
2. Their substance is kept: we know the technology, we adapt to the team rather than impose a
   method, we build a foundation the client can carry on with, and we have a track record.
